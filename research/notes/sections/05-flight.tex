\section*{Theorem 2. When a flight of stairs reaches its storey}
The game's rule (\texttt{nexel-worlds sim/climbing.py}, \texttt{flight}): a flight whose top step is at tile $t$, on
ground at height $h$, runs up to the first surface $s$ found scanning the four tiles beside $t$ in a fixed order and
each tile's surfaces from the lowest, such that $h+2\le s\le h+7$ and the two cells above $s$ are open. A design's
storey $j$ stands on a floor whose top is at $h+3j$; a wall of storey $j$ fills the cells above it; a door does not.
The flight to storey $k$ ($k\le2$, so $h+3k\le h+7$) has its top beside the door column $c$ of storey $k$, and the
other tiles beside $t$ have no surface in $[h+2,h+7]$ (the site is level around the build).
\begin{theorem} Laid at a given moment, the flight reaches storey $k$ if and only if, at that moment, the wall of every
storey $j<k$ stands in column $c$ and the cell of column $c$ at storey $k$ holds a door or nothing.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} The surfaces of column $c$ in $[h+2,h+7]$ are the floor tops $h+3j$, $1\le j\le k$; beside $t$ no other
tile has one. Scanned from the lowest, the flight stops at the first $h+3j$ whose cells above are open: open for
$j<k$ exactly when the wall of storey $j$ is not there, open at $j=k$ exactly when the cell holds a door or nothing.
So it stops at $h+3k$ iff every lower one is closed and that one is open.\end{proof}
\noindent Check. In the simulator, each multi-storey design stamped whole but its stairs, with one wall of the door
column below the flight's storey taken out or none, a flight laid from the planned stand (Table T2): with the column
standing, every flight reaches its storey; without the first storey's wall, the tower's top flight stops at storey 1.

\emph{So:} a builder must lay a flight only after its door column below stands, and must not count a cell as laid when
another piece shares its place. Ours did both wrong: its record of what it laid up a storey was keyed by place alone,
so the first floor's plank read as the wall standing on it; the wall never went up, the flight stopped at storey 1,
and the tower's top storey was out of reach from an empty inventory (it stood from a full one only because the order
happened to lay the wall first). With the record keyed by block or thing and each flight after its column, the tower
stands from an empty inventory on 2 of 3 seeds.
