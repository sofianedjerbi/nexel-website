\textbf{Trial 4: the judge checked the rules, not the request.} The design passed every rule and ignored the words.
The mind spent the hour on fibre: 111 plans, 0 of 15 goals done, holding the two beds it was gathering for, because its
text said what a bed takes but not that it owned two.

\textbf{Trial 5: the body undid the build.} The builder placed 750 pieces and none stood at the end: between build goals
the learned body and the gathering verb dug the site 463 times, down to a shaft 9 deep. The site was not protected from
the agent's own other activities.

\textbf{Trial 6: the reward got what it paid for.} 171 decisions of the mind, not one a build; 897 crafts an hour
(players: 1.7), 96\% repeats; the inventory at 0 wood and 0 fibre for the hour while the mind gathered them for a bed:
the body crafted 16 spears, 17 campfires and 7 bandanas from them. Two build descriptions the mind wrote were dropped,
since build words were kept only with the goal ``build''; 61\% of actions failed.

\textbf{Causes.} (i) \emph{Reward misspecification}: an achievement reward makes novelty the objective (E5 below proves
the goal is then never sought). (ii) \emph{No owner of long-horizon execution}: the mind replans every 20\,s and the body
acts between, so a build is never carried through; the systems that do build structures (Voyager, Mindcraft,
CraftUtopia) let a model plan and code execute to the end, and learned controllers (STEVE-1, GROOT, JARVIS-VLA) do short
skills, not structures. (iii) \emph{A test gap}: the builder's tests started with every piece in the inventory; the
live trial, an hour on a shared machine, was the only test of gathering. A fourth, smaller one: the trial 5 design was
infeasible anyway, its roof of stone blocks being a material the executor cannot obtain; the designer may choose what
the executor cannot do.
