\textbf{Trial 4: the judge checked the rules, not the request.} The design passed every rule of the game's judge and
ignored the words; a check that a design keeps what the words name (roof style, shape, storeys) was added. The language
model spent the hour on fibre (111 plans, 0 of 15 goals done; agent log) while holding the two beds it was gathering for:
its text said what a bed takes, not that it owned two.

\textbf{Trial 5: the agent undid its own build.} 750 placements, and 554 digs on tiles the agent itself had built on;
none of the design's 209 cells stood at the end (agent log). Between build goals the learned body and the gathering verb
treated the site as land like any other.

\textbf{Trial 6: nothing was built.} Of 171 goals the language model set, none was a build (agent log); two build
descriptions it wrote were dropped, because build words were kept only with the goal ``build''. Of 912 crafts, 751 were
building materials crafted by the old builder for its hut; the other 161 were things nothing asked for (17 campfires,
16 spears, 15 chairs, 7 bandanas, \dots), and they used the wood and fibre the language model was gathering for a bed:
the inventory read 0 of each for the hour.

\textbf{Trial 7: the executor builds.} The architect drew two houses on one site; the executor gathered the bill and
laid the first: a closed $3\times3$ room with bed, chest, campfire, table, chair and a door, under a roof. The game's
judge scores it 6/6 for working and 8/8 for sense (the best real player's room in that world: 4/6 and 5/8), 4/8 for
looks (mixed wall materials, no windows, no path). Unasked crafts fell to 17 (161 in trial 6). Two faults stopped the
second house: the language model's requests failed 75 times from minute 19 (not logged; failures are now written
with their cause), and with the mind silent the learned body acted freely, placing 22 workbenches and digging 134 times
on tiles it had built on: trial 7 ran before the body was restricted (\S The system).

\textbf{Causes.} (i) \emph{The learned body's reward} pays $+1$ the first time each of $\sim$150 events happens in an
episode, as in Crafter [Hafner 2022]; it acts whenever no goal steers it, and nothing in that reward values a design standing, so building
and unbuilding are alike to it [Amodei et al.\ 2016]. (ii) \emph{No owner of long-horizon execution}: the language model
replans every $\sim$20\,s and the body acts between. Agents that do build structures let a model plan and code execute to
the end [Wang et al.\ 2023; White et al.\ 2025; CraftUtopia 2026]; learned controllers do short skills [Lifshitz et al.\ 2023;
Cai et al.\ 2024; JARVIS-VLA 2025]. (iii)
\emph{A test gap}: the builder's tests started with every piece in hand; only the hour-long live trial tested gathering.
(iv) The trial 5 design was infeasible regardless: its roof of stone blocks is a material no verb can obtain; the
designer may choose what the executor cannot do.
