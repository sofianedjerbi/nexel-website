\section*{S. Rewarding verified progress}
Let $\Phi(s)$ be the share of the design's cells standing, read from the game, $\Phi=0$ at the end of an episode, and
shape the reward with $F(s,a,s')=\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$.
\begin{theorem}[Ng, Harada, Russell 1999] For every policy, $Q'^{\pi}=Q^\pi-\Phi$; so the optimal policies under $r+F$
are those under $r$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Along any trajectory from $s_0$, $\sum_t\gamma^tF(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})=\sum_t(\gamma^{t+1}\Phi(s_{t+1})-\gamma^t\Phi(s_t))$
telescopes to $-\Phi(s_0)$, the last term vanishing ($\Phi=0$ at the end, or $\gamma^t\to0$). Taking expectations,
$Q'^\pi(s,a)=Q^\pi(s,a)-\Phi(s)$; a term depending on $s$ only does not change $\arg\max_a$.\end{proof}
\noindent Check. A build task: a line of 8 places, blocks taken at one end, a site of 4 cells at the other; reward 1 when
the last cell is filled; actions move, take, place, and dig (a filled cell back to a block). Solved exactly, the shaped
values equal the sparse ones minus $\Phi$ to $10^{-6}$, and digging a standing cell pays less than 0 in every state.
Q-learning ($\epsilon=0.1$, step 0.5, 100 steps an episode), episodes until the greedy policy builds the site: shaped,
$103$ (IQM, 95\% CI $[93, 118]$); sparse, not within 3\,000 on any of 20 seeds, the same seeds (Figure S2).

\emph{So:} reward the body for cells of the design standing, read from the game: what was optimal stays optimal, undoing
the build loses reward at once, and the signal comes at every step instead of after hundreds. The same holds for any
verified progress (items gathered toward a bill, distance to a target).
