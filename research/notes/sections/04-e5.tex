\section*{E5. The reward decides what is done}
\begin{proposition} If the goal pays $0$ (achievements only), every policy that never reaches it is optimal.\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Reaching the goal adds $0$ to any return and ends the episode, so it can only lose achievements still to
come: never reaching it is at least as good.\end{proof}
\noindent Check. An exact dynamic program: 6 craft spots of 2 steps beside the start, the goal $d$ steps off, 16 steps.
Achievements only: the goal is never reached. The verified goal only: it is. Both mixed: reached in $76\%$ of 25
settings of craft pay and distance, not when crafts pay $\ge0.2$ and the goal is far (Figure E5). Live, the body paid
per first craft made 897 crafts an hour and no house.

\emph{So:} pay only what the game verifies done; a mix keeps the old pull wherever it outweighs the goal.
