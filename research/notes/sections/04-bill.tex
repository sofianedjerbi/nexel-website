\section*{Theorem 1. The least raw material a design takes}
A recipe table: each craftable item $k$ has one recipe, taking $m_{k,c}$ of each input $c$, yielding $y_k\ge1$, and
possibly a station $\sigma_k$ (a workbench), used but not consumed; land items $L$ (wood, stone, fibre) are gathered,
never crafted; the graph $k\to c$ (inputs and station) is acyclic. Given a demand $D$ (what the design's unlaid cells
take) and an inventory $I$, process the items in a topological order that puts every item after all items that consume
it, with $\mathit{need}_k=D_k+\sum_p m_{p,k}x_p+[\,k=\sigma_p$ for some $p$ with $x_p>0$, first time$\,]$ and
$$x_k=\Big\lceil\frac{(\mathit{need}_k-I_k)^+}{y_k}\Big\rceil\ (k\notin L),\qquad B_k=(\mathit{need}_k-I_k)^+\ (k\in L).$$
\begin{theorem} (i) From $I+B$, crafting each $k$ $x_k$ times, inputs first, leaves $D$ in hand. (ii) Every raw bill
$B'$ such that $I+B'$ suffices has $B'\ge B$ in every item. (iii) Expanding each cell on its own instead costs up to
$y_k$ times as much of $k$'s inputs: $\sum_{\text{cells}}\lceil 1/y_k\rceil$ against $\lceil n/y_k\rceil$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} (i) Taken inputs first, item $k$'s supply is $I_k+y_kx_k\ge\mathit{need}_k$, which covers its consumers'
crafts and $D_k$; a station is made once and stands for every craft at it. (ii) Take any sufficient plan. By
induction along the order (consumers first), it crafts each $k\notin L$ at least $x_k$ times: when $k$ is reached,
each consumer $p$ is crafted at least $x_p$ times (hypothesis), taking at least $\sum_pm_{p,k}x_p$ of $k$, $D_k$ must
remain, and $k$ comes only from $I_k$ and its one recipe, $y_k$ a craft; a station needed by some craft must exist
at least once. So the plan holds at least $\mathit{need}_k$ of every item; for $k\in L$ that comes from $I_k+B'_k$,
hence $B'_k\ge B_k$. (iii) $\sum_{i=1}^n\lceil1/y\rceil=n\ge\lceil n/y\rceil$, the ratio tending to $y$.\end{proof}
\noindent Check. The executor had expanded cell by cell: a gabled house's 107 shingles (2 per wood) counted 107 wood,
54 do; its whole bill falls from 181 wood and 6 stone to 104 and 4. Built from exactly $B$, with no gathering, 14 of 15
design-seeds stand whole (Table T1); 7 end with nothing left. The leftovers are wood the builder got clearing trees
off the site (seed 9: 6 gained, 108 used, 4 above $B$: the builder is not optimal, the bound is). The one failure,
the round home on seed 9, was a builder stall since fixed (\S executor), not a short bill.

\emph{So:} the executor gathers exactly what the design needs: no trip wasted on a wrong count, nothing short.
