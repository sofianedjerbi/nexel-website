\section*{A build owned by code, checked from an empty inventory}
\textbf{Executor.} A drawn design's unlaid cells are expanded through the game's recipes down to what the land gives
(a crafting station counted once); the raw material is gathered first, in trips of 24 (a trip, not a walk per piece),
then the builder lays the design with a full inventory, the case its tests already covered. While a design is
unfinished the executor chooses every action; the learned body acts only when the agent is hurt. The mind's build words
are kept whatever its goal, until it names another build or none.

\textbf{Acceptance test.} The game's simulator, an empty inventory, a design drawn: the executor alone must make it stand.
Six designs (a gabled house, two storeys, a round house, a three-storey tower, a T and a cross of wings with mixed
roofs) on three seeds each: 12 of 18 stand whole ($67\%$, Wilson 95\% interval $44$--$84\%$), in 335 to 7\,828 decisions (Figure S). The builder fetching a craft's
worth at a time laid a fifth of the gabled house in 5\,000 decisions.

\textbf{What the test exposed}, each a defect the live trials could only have shown as ``nothing built'':
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{A side effect in a legality check.} Computing the action mask asked the builder for its next step, and the
builder re-sited the house when the agent was more than 64 tiles away: each gathering trip moved the house. Legality
predicates must be pure.
\item \emph{Oscillation.} Fetching per craft, the agent walked between the trees and the site, each out of view of the
other, for 1\,500 steps. Gathering the whole bill first removes it.
\item \emph{Geometry the site check missed.} A roof's overhang over a hill puts a cell inside the land: placed for
ever, refused for ever. The site check now bounds the land's height under every overhanging cell.
\item \emph{Counting.} A flight of stairs was one piece in the plan but takes one stairs item per step: upper storeys
were unreachable.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Still failing.} The tower on all three seeds: its top storey is not reached from an empty inventory though
it is from a full one; the order cells are laid in matters and nothing plans it. Three more runs stall: the house on seed 21,
the two-storey house and the T on seed 7; seed 21 fails or nearly fails twice, which points at the map
rather than the design. Earth and stone-block roofs need material dug from the land, which neither verb does. Eighteen
runs on three seeds can only rank designs coarsely; the live trial of the executor is pending.
