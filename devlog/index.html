Devlog

How Nexel Worlds got made, a few days at a time. Short and honest: what worked, what did not, what we measured. The full day-by-day notes live in the code repo's JOURNAL.md.

  • Inside Rook's head

    2026-11-23

    A page that shows what an AI player sees, thinks, decides and remembers. Watch 13 real minutes of Rook.

  • AI players: from a language model to learning

    2026-11-23

    The game was built for AI players. Making one that plays well is the hard part. Two tries so far.

  • The jump, eight times

    2026-11-23

    A jump looks simple. Ours needed eight versions, and real frames to judge them.

  • Water that flows

    2026-11-23

    Water used to stay where the world put it. Now it runs, a cell at a time.

  • Tests that tell the truth

    2026-11-22

    Hundreds of tests in a real browser, and what made them flaky. Hint: it was rarely load.

  • The HD voxel look

    2026-11-21

    From flat tiles to real blocks with a horizon, light and shadows. The first audit said no.

  • Floors, tunnels, and 512 blocks down

    2026-11-18

    Houses with storeys, stairs dug into the ground, and a world 1024 blocks tall.

  • Seen through the rock, a name, and houses block by block

    2026-11-15

    An x-ray view for when the land hides you, black tiles on one phone, the name Nexel Worlds, and real building.

  • Hills, holes, and water you can see into

    2026-11-12

    The flat world got heights, a way underground, and clear water. The underground took six tries.

  • The whole game in a browser tab

    2026-11-09

    The server is plain Python, so we ran it inside the page. Then on a phone.

  • Day one: a world for robots, then a game

    2026-11-08

    It started as a server for AI players. By the evening it had to be a game people want to play.
